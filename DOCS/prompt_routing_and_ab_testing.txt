Smart routing in a multi-model system: rather than calling every available model for every 
prompt, a router inspects the prompt and selects the model or models best suited to it. A 
simple, interpretable approach uses keyword and pattern heuristics rather than a trained 
classifier, which keeps the system fast, free to run, and easy to explain to a reviewer or 
interviewer.

Typical routing categories: short factual questions (who, what, when) can be answered well
by small, fast, cheap models. Code-related prompts benefit from models with stronger code 
training. Long, open-ended, or comparative prompts that require multi-step reasoning benefit 
from larger flagship models, even at higher latency and cost.

Cost and latency tradeoffs: a flagship model generally produces higher-quality answers on 
complex tasks but costs more per token and responds more slowly. A fast, cheap model is 
often just as accurate on simple factual questions, so routing simple queries away from the 
flagship model reduces average cost without meaningfully reducing answer quality.

A/B prompt testing: two variants of a prompt (differing in wording, added instructions, or 
examples) are run against the same model, and the resulting answers are compared side by 
side. This isolates the effect of prompt wording from model choice, which is useful for 
iteratively improving system prompts before deploying them.

Interpreting an A/B result: differences in factual accuracy usually matter most; differences 
in verbosity, tone, or formatting are often just stylistic and may not indicate one prompt 
is objectively better than the other for all use cases.
